## ABSTRACT

Background: Menstrual cycle irregularities have been linked to several health hazards; it is unclear how these risks relate to cardiovascular disease (CVD). The purpose of this meta-analysis was to compile data regarding the

relationship between irregular menstruation and women’s cardiovascular and metabolic outcomes. Methods: A systematic search of online databases was conducted for studies comparing cardiovascular and metabolic outcomes between women with irregular and regular menstrual cycles. Pooled risk ratios (RR) and 95

% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated adopting a random effects model.

Results: Eight observational studies comprising 354,419 women were included. Women with irregular menstrual cycles showed a significantly increased risk of overall cardiovascular disease (RR: 1.43, 95 % CI: 1.01 to 2.03) compared to those with regular menstrual cycles. Subgroup analyses for specific cardiovascular outcomes demonstrated increased risks of coronary artery disease (RR: 1.29, 95 % CI: 1.14–1.46), hypertension (RR: 1.50, 95 % CI: 1.23–1.82) and heart failure (RR: 1.45, 95 % CI: 1.11–1.89) in women with irregular cycles compared to those with regular cycles. Additionally, the risk of cerebrovascular disease was higher in women with irregular cycles compared to those with regular cycles (RR: 1.62, 95 % CI: 1.10–2.40), Notably, patients with irregular menstrual cycles were found to have a higher risk of developing diabetes mellitus (RR: 1.75, 95 % CI: 1.31–2.34) and hypercholesterolemia (RR: 1.33, 95 % CI: 1.16–1.52).

## Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVD) are among the most common causes of worldwide morbidity and mortality [1] The number of CVD in 1990 was 271 million cases and kept increasing over years to reach 523 million cases which is almost the double in 2019 [2]. Investigating their related risk factors is important to decrease the current increasing CVD incidence. Hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, smoking, obesity, and family history are some of these factors based on previous studies [3]. Moreover, menstrual cycle characteristics, particularly irregularity and cycle length, have also been indicated as possible factors. This is due to the underlying hormonal imbalances or the metabolic disturbances that can lead to CVD risk in women [4,5]. Cycle irregularity refers to the varying timing of menstrual periods from month to month. This can include infrequent periods (oligomenorrhea), very frequent periods (polymenorrhagia), or highly variable cycle lengths. A typical cycle is 21–35 days in length and lasts for two to seven days. However, the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists indicated 35 days as the typical menstrual cycle for most women; but this varies from study to study [6].

Nevertheless, previous studies have suggested associations between cycle irregularities and various cardiovascular risk factors including obesity, smoking, and stress [7]. Irregular cycles have been linked to insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, and polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) all of which may increase cardiovascular risk [8,9]. Addition­ ally, another study has found associations between long menstrual cy­ cles (>38 days) and increased risk of type 2 diabetes [10]. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

However, the correlation between the cycle characteristics and CVD outcomes is still ambiguous, with conflicting results in the literature. While a few studies have reported higher risks of coronary heart disease and cardiovascular mortality associated with irregular or relatively longer cycles [4,5,11], others reported it as insignificant associations [12,13]. Variations in study designs, populations, definitions of irregu­ larity, and assessed CVD outcomes have contributed to the inconsistent findings. To this end, this meta-analysis aims to synthesize the available evidence on the association between menstrual cycle irregularities and CVD risk among women by pooling data from multiple studies which can provide a comprehensive assessment of this relationship and help in the identification of women at higher risk of CVD earlier in life with implementation of preventive strategies. ## Methods

Cochrane Collaboration Handbook for Systematic Reviews of In­ terventions was followed in order to conduct this systematic review and meta-analysis. We also followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Sys­ tematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) Statement guidelines [14]. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

## Data collection

We conducted a systematic literature search using Web of Science, Scopus, and PubMed databases on August 1, 2024. No restrictions were placed on publication date or participant age. The search strategy included terms related to menstrual irregularities (e.g., “irregular menstrual cycles,” “short menstrual cycles,” “long menstrual cycles”) and cardiovascular disease outcomes. The detailed search strategy can be found in (Supplementary File). Covidence was used to remove du­ plicates and perform the study selection process. ## Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Observational studies (cohort, case-control, or cross-sectional de­ signs) were eligible for inclusion if they reported on the association between menstrual irregularities and cardiovascular disease risk. Only studies that reported raw data that is necessary for the analysis were included,. Primary outcomes of interest included composite cardiovas­ cular disease, coronary artery disease, cerebrovascular disease, and heart failure. Secondary outcomes included cardiovascular mortality, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and hypercholesterolemia. For this study, menstrual irregularity was defined as abnormalities in frequency and length of cycles (consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days).

Case reports, case-series as well as non-human studies were excluded. In addition, studies that did not provide raw quantitative data needed for the analysis (e.g., events and totals) for both regular and irregular groups were excluded. Conference abstracts, books, and edi­ torials were also excluded.

## Data extraction

Two independent authors extracted data from eligible studies using a preformed organized Excel sheet. The following information was collected: author, year of publication, country, study design, follow-up duration, sample size, participant characteristics (age, race/ethnicity, BMI), menstrual cycle status (irregular vs. regular) cardiovascular out­ comes (e.g., CVD, coronary artery disease, hypertension, heart failure, cerebrovascular disease) and metabolic data (hypercholesterolemia and diabetes).

## Quality assessment

The studies’ quality was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for cohort and case-control studies, considering factors such as selection bias, comparability of groups, and outcome assessment. Studies were considered good quality if they received 3 or 4 stars in the selection domain, 1 or 2 stars in the comparability domain, and 2 or 3 stars in the outcome/exposure domain. Fair quality was defined as 2 stars in the selection domain, 1 or 2 stars in the comparability domain, and 2 or 3 stars in the outcome/exposure domain. Poor quality was defined as 0 or 1 star in the selection domain, 0 stars in the comparability domain, or 0 or 1 star in the outcome/exposure domain [15]. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

## Statistical analysis

Dichotomous data was presented as frequency and percentage while continuous data was presented as mean and standard deviation (SD). We performed a random-effects meta-analysis to estimate the pooled effect size for the association between menstrual irregularities and cardio­ vascular disease risk. Results were presented as risk ratios with 95 % confidence intervals. The result is considered significant when p-value is less than 0.05. Heterogeneity across studies were assessed using the I2 statistic and Cochran’s Q test. Statistical analysis was performed using the ’meta’ package (version 7.0–0) in R (version 4.3.0) [16]. ## Discussion

This meta-analysis provides a comprehensive examination of the

association between menstrual cycle irregularities and cardiovascular health in women. Our findings suggest a complex relationship between menstrual irregularities and various cardiovascular and metabolic out­ comes. The overall relative risk of cardiovascular disease was signifi­ cantly higher in women with irregular menstrual cycles ((RR: 1.43, 95 % CI: 1.01 to 2.03). Also, by performing subgroup analysis on each CVD type, we found strong associations between irregular menstruation and coronary artery disease (RR: 1.29), heart failure (RR: 1.45), hyperten­ sion (RR: 1.50). Finally, we found increased risks of cerebrovascular disease (RR: 1.62, 95 % CI: 1.10 to 2.40), and metabolic disorders like diabetes mellitus (RR: 1.75) and hypercholesterolemia (RR: 1.33).

This trend aligns with previous research suggesting that menstrual

irregularities may be an indicator of underlying hormonal imbalances or

This highlights the importance of early intervention and comprehensive care in women presenting with menstrual irregularities. The strengths of this meta-analysis include its comprehensive nature, incorporating studies from diverse populations and settings as it is considered the first systematic review and meta-analysis to investigate the association between menstrual cycle irregularities and CVD risks. The inclusion of both cohort and case-control studies allowed for a broader perspective on the associations of interest. However, several limitations should be noted. The heterogeneity in definitions of men­ strual irregularities across studies may have influenced our results. Additionally, the observational nature of the included studies limits our ability to establish causal relationships. Finally, the incomplete and inconsistent reporting of baseline cardiometabolic risk factors in the included studies is another limitation. Some studies reported lipid pa­ rameters (e.g., total cholesterol or prevalence of hypercholesterolemia), while others provided no lipid data. Physical activity was variably assessed using continuous measures, categorical classifications, or not reported at all, and dietary information was rarely available. Conse­ quently, we could not include these factors in pooled analyses, despite their established role in cardiovascular risk. Future research should standardize reporting of lipid profiles, dietary patterns, and physical activity to clarify their role as potential mediators or confounders in the link between menstrual irregularities and cardiometabolic outcomes. Our findings have important clinical implications for women’s health. Menstrual cycle characteristics, often overlooked in cardiovas­ cular risk assessment, may serve as early indicators of cardiometabolic risk. Healthcare providers should consider incorporating menstrual history into cardiovascular risk stratification for women. Future research should focus on elucidating the mechanisms underlying the observed associations, particularly the link between menstrual irregu­ larities and cerebrovascular disease. Longitudinal studies with stan­ dardized definitions of menstrual irregularities and comprehensive assessment of cardiovascular outcomes are needed to further clarify

these relationships.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, this meta-analysis provides evidence for an association between menstrual cycle irregularities and increased risk of various cardiovascular and metabolic outcomes. These findings underscore the importance of considering reproductive factors in assessing and man­

aging cardiovascular health in women across their lifespan.
